# Legitimate Fronts and Criminal Logistics

**Status: noncanonical design proposal.** Matt accepted this idea for preservation on 2026-09-23.
It is not a settled rule, a roadmap commitment, or authorization to implement. The *Gangsters:
Organized Crime* research and the laundry/superlab example are inspiration, not specifications.

## Feature intent

Legitimate enterprises can give an illicit operation practical capabilities as well as financial
cover. Concealing production and moving its output at scale are different problems. A laundry or
similar facility might offer space, staff, and plausible activity around production; a freight or
logistics business might offer vehicles, routes, workers, storage, and paperwork for distribution.
One front should not automatically solve both problems.

This develops `GAME_VISION.md`'s settled direction that businesses can offer operational
opportunities and financial cover, rather than existing only as laundering meters. The particular
front and logistics mechanics below remain proposals.

## Working hypotheses

- A small illicit operation may be possible without a legitimate front. Its volume, reach, and
  concealment could be constrained by the people and facilities actually available; a universal
  front requirement is not decided.
- Expansion may create a distribution bottleneck: a few crew members cannot necessarily carry the
  same quantity, frequency, or geographic reach as a functioning logistics network.
- The player could build, buy, borrow, partner for, or infiltrate useful business capacity. Access
  need not mean ownership, and another actor may control the relationship.
- Benefits and liabilities should follow concrete capabilities and activity, not a generic
  "front" or concealment bonus. Workers, shipments, records, and routes can also create witnesses,
  dependencies, costs, and investigative leads.
- The player and other actors should know only what their position and information sources allow;
  a business's legitimate appearance does not reveal the illicit operation beneath it.

## Questions for later design

- Which early criminal activities need premises or distribution at all, and at what scale does
  dedicated infrastructure become worth its cost?
- How do capacity, reliability, exposure, and legitimate revenue interact without creating a
  detailed logistics simulation before one is needed?
- What can employees, partners, competitors, and investigators observe or infer from operations?
- How do shared or borrowed fronts change trust, leverage, ownership, and vulnerability?
- Which first playable example would prove that a front changes a meaningful decision rather than
  merely adding a required purchase?

Keep the *Gangsters* traces as comparative research. Reopen an unanswered trace only when a
specific Crime Empire design decision depends on how that game's mechanic actually works.
