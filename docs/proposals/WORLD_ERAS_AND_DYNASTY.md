# World Eras and Dynasty

**Status: noncanonical long-term design proposal.** Canon settles persistent consequences,
succession, continuous calendar time, and the possibility of state-scale play. It does not settle a
1910s start, a century-long campaign, an end date, or the required historical content.

## Proposed direction

A campaign could begin in the 1910s and continue through multiple generations in one persistent
world. Characters age, die, retire, inherit, defect, return from prison, or become part of remembered
history. Control may pass through a viable heir, trusted subordinate, spouse, protégé, rival, or
another eligible character without transferring the predecessor's personal knowledge or statistics.

Historical change should alter the opportunity and information environment rather than function as a
simple technology unlock tree. Across decades:

- communications become faster and create different records;
- cameras, databases, financial reporting, forensic practice, and institutional coordination change
  what traces exist and who can connect them;
- transport, markets, industries, borders, and supply routes create or destroy criminal opportunities;
- policing, prosecution, incarceration, politics, and public media change institutional behavior;
- organization cultures may adapt, fragment, professionalize, or resist a new era;
- younger and older characters may disagree about acceptable methods, identity, and risk.

No era grants omniscience. Technology changes trace production, access, retention, search,
attribution, and sharing; institutions and individuals still need authority, resources, competence,
and legitimate information paths.

## Long-term expansion concept: cyberpunk conspiracy era

**Status: noncanonical DLC idea, not a roadmap commitment.** A future alternate or later-era
expansion could apply the same character, organization, information, and institutional foundations to
a cyberpunk criminal ecosystem. The era should change opportunity and surveillance rather than
replace the base game with unrelated mechanics.

Appropriate criminal activity could include data theft, cyber-extortion, identity and credential
markets, corporate espionage, black-market technology or augmentation, synthetic-drug supply,
surveillance manipulation, information brokerage, infrastructure sabotage, and political influence
operations. Each activity would still need actors, capabilities, access, traces, witnesses or
records, information paths, and persistent consequences; "cybercrime" must not become an abstract
button that bypasses the simulation.

A cult or cult-like organization could operate across politics, legitimate institutions, and the
underworld. A player might build, infiltrate, finance, exploit, expose, investigate, fragment, or
destroy it. Cult influence should arise through individual recruitment, belief, coercion, patronage,
compromise, secrecy, favors, and offices rather than a global cult-power meter. Members, fronts,
politicians, police, businesspeople, and criminals may each know different parts of the network and
interpret its purpose differently.

The investigative side could support noir conspiracy play: connect observations that initially seem
unrelated, cultivate or pressure sources, compare conflicting testimony, uncover institutional
protection, seek authorization, survive interference, and decide whether an apparently unified
conspiracy is real, exaggerated, fragmented, or being used by somebody else. Supernatural certainty
is not implied; ambiguity, belief, ritual, fraud, sincere conviction, and deliberate mythmaking can
coexist without the interface revealing an omniscient answer.

This expansion idea is valuable only if it reuses the base game's actor-neutral systems. The same
world should permit manipulating the organization for power, serving it, resisting it from within,
or investigating it from an institutional or independent position. It does not authorize alternate
careers, a cult system, a cyberpunk era, or DLC production now.

## Scope guardrail

The first playable does not need a century of authored content or several complete technology
regimes. Architecture should not encode the 1910s proposal as a current hierarchy or compatibility
layer. The direction earns implementation only after a shorter run proves that time, imprisonment,
loss, succession, and persistent history already create worthwhile play.

## Potential milestone slices, not a sequence

1. One succession that preserves the world while changing controlled-character knowledge and
   relationships.
2. One absence interval—prison, incapacity, exile, or retirement—during which other characters and
   operations continue and leave a legible history.
3. One bounded technology or institutional regime change that alters trace production or discovery
   without special-casing the player.
4. One later-era organization adapting differently because of its people, offices, and culture.

## Falsifiers

- A successor must not inherit the predecessor's beliefs, private relationships, or hidden knowledge.
- Advancing through an absence must produce the same scheduled world history regardless of render or
  fast-forward rate.
- A surveillance-era change must alter a named trace/observation/access path, not add a global heat
  multiplier.
- Historical change must create at least one different decision or consequence; renamed art and dates
  alone do not prove an era system.

## Human rulings before implementation

- Is the 1910s a canonical starting date or only one campaign preset?
- What maximum campaign span remains legible and producible?
- Which historical changes materially alter decisions rather than add content burden?
- Which succession relationships are eligible, and how is the next viewpoint chosen?
